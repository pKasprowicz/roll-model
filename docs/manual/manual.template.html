Roll Model

User manual for version {{VERSION}} ({{LABEL}}) · built {{DATE}}

Made with AIRoll Model, and this manual, were created with extensive use of AI: most of the code, tests, documentation and rudiment transcriptions were written by AI models (Anthropic's Claude), directed and reviewed by the author. The program has not been independently audited and parts have never been tried on real hardware (the Roland V31, the controller presets). Expect mistakes, and check anything that matters.

1. Welcome

What it does

Roll Model is a desktop practice tool for drummers with an electronic kit. It does three things:

Shows the music

Loads a MusicXML drum score, or one of the 40 drum rudiments, and draws it on a percussion staff.

Listens to you

Captures what you play on a Roland V31 (or any e-kit with MIDI) and draws your hits directly under the written notes, on the same time axis.

Grades you

Compares your hits with the notes: accuracy, timing, consistency, tempo drift, wrong pads, extra notes, dynamics, and for rudiments, how even your two hands are.

You can also listen to anything before you play it, with a built-in drum kit or your module's own sounds, and practise against a free metronome. A control surface (Behringer X-Touch Mini or Compact, or any Mackie Control device) can run the program while your hands hold sticks.

Quick start

  1. Connect the kit. Plug the V31 into the computer by USB and start Roll Model. The program connects to the first MIDI input whose name contains V31; the top right corner shows a green dot and the port name when it worked (see Connecting your kit). A small splash window says what the program is doing while it starts (Starting the program).
  2. Open a score or a rudiment. Click Open MusicXML… or drop a file on the window, or press Practise the 40 rudiments on the Home tab.
  3. Listen first. Press L to hear what you are about to play, with the count-in and click.
  4. Record. Press R. After the count-in, play. Your hits appear under the notes as you play and the take stops by itself at the end.
  5. Read the result. Green notes were right. The Results tab explains everything else.
First timeRun Settings → Timing → Calibrate once (Latency and calibration). It removes the delay of your sound card and MIDI path, so that a note you play exactly on the click is graded as exactly on time.

Installing

Roll Model is a single program; there is no installer.

SystemWhat to do
WindowsUnzip the release archive and run roll-model.exe. Nothing else is needed (Windows MIDI and WASAPI audio are used).
LinuxUnpack the archive and run roll-model. The ALSA libraries must be present (libasound2); building from source needs libasound2-dev and pkg-config.
macOSNo extra requirements (CoreMIDI and CoreAudio). Build from source with Rust.

The archive also holds this manual (roll-model-manual.html), a developer guide (roll-model-developer-guide.html) for anyone who wants to change the program, the licence and third-party notices, the README, the changelog and the examples folder with a sample score. To open a score at start-up, give its path as the only argument: roll-model examples/basic_rock_beat.musicxml.

Building from source

Install a recent Rust from rustup.rs (1.85 is the oldest that can read the dependencies; 1.99 is the version it is tested with), then:

cargo run --release                                  # start empty
cargo run --release -- examples/basic_rock_beat.musicxml
cargo test                                           # the unit tests
cargo build --release --no-default-features          # without the sound card (drops cpal)

Without the sound card the click and Listen can still be sent to a MIDI output.

2. Connecting your kit

The Roland V31

  • Connect the module by USB, or through a MIDI interface on the module's MIDI OUT. The program connects automatically to the first input port whose name contains V31. Otherwise choose the port on Settings → MIDI input.
  • On Windows a MIDI port can be opened by one program at a time. Close your DAW, drum software or any other MIDI monitor first.
  • The monitor on Settings → MIDI input lists each incoming note with its velocity and the instrument it was mapped to. Hit every pad and zone once to see that each one is recognised.
  • The default pad map is the common Roland V-Drums factory map:

Not verifiedThis map is the factory map of Roland V-Drums in general. It has not been checked against a V31 itself. If your module is set up differently, use Learn (below).

Changing the map: Learn

On Settings → MIDI input, every instrument has a text field with its note numbers and a Learn button. Press Learn, then hit the pad or zone: the note now belongs to that instrument only. You can also type note numbers separated by commas. Reset to Roland V31 defaults restores the table.

Hi-hat pedal

The pedal (MIDI CC#4) tells the program whether the hi-hat was closed or open when you hit it. A stick hit on the closed or open hi-hat note counts as closed when the pedal is at least three quarters of the way down, otherwise as open. Until the first pedal message arrives, the note number alone decides.

If your pedal never reports the full 0–127 range, lower Hi-hat pedal value when closed on Settings → MIDI input: close the pedal, hit the hi-hat and read the pedal value in the monitor, then enter that value. Roland's convention (127 = closed) is assumed.

No kit at hand

Tick Settings → General → Computer keyboard plays pads and the keys act as pads: Z kick, X snare, G cross-stick, C / V / B hi-hat closed / open / pedal, A S D F toms 1–4, Q crash 1, W ride, E crash 2.

Good for testing onlyKey presses are time-stamped when the window handles them, about 16 ms late and uneven. That is fine for trying the program, not for judging your timing.

3. A tour of the window

Starting the program

The window opens at once with a small splash window of its own in front of it: the name, the version and the copyright, and a list of what the program is doing while it starts: reading the settings, looking for MIDI ports, connecting the drum module, connecting the controller, listing the sound outputs and, if you gave a file on the command line, opening it. Each finished step stays on the list with what it found; something that is not there (the drum module, the controller, a sound output) shows in amber, so you can see at a glance why the top bar shows red dots.

When everything is done the splash stays, so you can read it, and says Ready. It closes when you press any key or click the mouse in the program (the key or click that closes it is not used for anything else, and nothing closes it in the first moments after the start). The main window is empty until the steps are done; on a slow start (a MIDI driver that takes seconds to open) the list shows which step it is waiting for.

Top bar

  • Open MusicXML… loads a score; Reload reads the same file again (after editing it in your notation program).
  • Tabs: Home, Score, Rudiments, Metronome, Results, Settings. Score is greyed until a score is loaded.
  • Right side: the controller (if one is chosen) with its layer, the last drum hit (instrument, note, velocity) and a dot for the MIDI input: green with the port name when connected, red with no MIDI input when not. A click on the controller opens its help screen.

Status line

The bottom line shows the latest message: what was loaded, the result of a take, a controller change. Problems appear there in red with a Dismiss button.

Mouse wheel on sliders

Every slider follows the mouse wheel: hover it and scroll to change the value one step per notch. The page does not scroll while you do it. The controls above the score are drawn larger than the rest because they are used with the mouse from the drum stool.

4. Home

The Home tab is where the program starts. It has the Open MusicXML… button, a button that takes you to the Rudiments, and the list of recent scores (the last ten). Click an entry to open it; the × removes it from the list and Clear list empties it. A file that no longer exists is greyed out and marked “file not found”. You can also drop .musicxml, .xml or .mxl files on the window.

5. The Score tab

Loading a score

Export the drum part from MuseScore, Guitar Pro, Sibelius, Dorico or similar as MusicXML and open it. The status line reports the title, the number of measures and notes and the tempo. Warnings from the import (for example a missing tempo marking) are shown in amber under the title. See MusicXML scores for what is supported.

Range and tempo

ControlWhat it does
Measures from / toThe bars to practise. You can also click a bar to start there, and Shift+click to set the end. The chosen range has a blue background.
tempo25–150 % of the written tempo, with the resulting BPM next to it. The score's own tempo changes are respected.

Range and tempo are not saved; they start fresh with each score.

Listen

L or the Listen button plays exactly what a take would ask of you: the selected bars at the chosen tempo with the same count-in and click. Accents are louder than normal strokes, ghost notes softer, grace notes land just before the beat. A moving playhead follows along and the label next to the button shows the bar and beat (or the count-in). Press L again or Esc to stop.

If you change the range or the tempo while it plays, Listen restarts after a moment (without the count-in). The playhead follows the clock, not the sound: a sound card with a large buffer plays a few tens of milliseconds later. See Sound and instrument mixer for what you hear.

Record

  1. Choose the bars and the tempo.
  2. Press R. The count-in plays (the number of clicks left is shown big), then ⏺ REC appears.
  3. Play. Each hit is drawn on the “You” staff as it happens and coloured as soon as it is matched to a note. The line above the score shows the running grade.
  4. The take ends by itself one second after the last note, or press Esc. Notes after the point where you stopped are left ungraded.

Starting without the mouse. With the start trigger switched on, two hits in a row (by default a crash, then a tom, within half a second) start the recording as R does, so you can begin with the sticks in your hands. It works on the Score and Rudiments tabs.

Range, tempo and score are locked while a take records. Every finished take is added to the session history on the Results tab.

Reading the colours

ColourOn a written noteOn one of your hits
GreenPerfect: within 20 ms of the note (adjustable)
Light greenGood: within 45 ms
AmberOK: inside the hit window but further out
OrangeRight time, wrong pad or zone (for example an open instead of a closed hi-hat)
RedMissed (no hit found)Extra (a hit that matches no note)

A hit placed left of its note was early, right of it late. Hover a hit to see its exact offset in milliseconds. The small bars under your staff show velocity, the beat lines show where the beats are.

Layout and zoom

  • zoom (0.5–3) sets how wide the bars are drawn when the rows are automatic; with a fixed number of bars per row it is greyed out. follow scrolls the score along with the playhead during playback and recording.
  • Layout opens three settings. Bars per row: auto fits as many as the width allows; a fixed number (1–8) gives every bar the same width so barlines line up from row to row (and the zoom no longer matters). If that many bars do not fit, fewer are shown and a note says so. Start a row at bar forces a given bar to begin a row; with 4 bars per row and a row starting at bar 2 you get [1] [2–5] [6–9] …. New row after a pause (on by default): a bar that ends with a pause is the last of its row, and the score goes on in the next row. This wins over bars per row: after a pause a new row starts and the bars are counted from there. A bar counts as ending with a pause when a rest runs to the barline and no note in any voice starts during it. A rest under the notes of another voice (the feet resting while the hands play) is not a pause, and a run of empty bars is one pause, so the row ends after the last of them.
  • Each score keeps its own layout: open it again and its rows come back (a score you never laid out keeps the settings on screen). The last 30 scores are remembered; the automatic layout is not stored.
  • The bar number in these settings is the position in the score as shown. With repeats expanded, the numbers printed above the bars can repeat; the Layout menu shows the printed number of the chosen bar.
NotationThe staff has proportional spacing: a note's place is its time in the bar, so the hits you play line up with it. Eighths and shorter notes of a beat are joined by beams (the hands and the feet separately, as two voices), a lone short note has flags, triplets, sextuplets and quintuplets have a bracket with a number, and the rests the file prints are drawn (the hands' voice high, the feet's low; a rest that would sit on a note moves out of its way). The score does not store how long a note lasts, so a note counts as lasting until the next one of its voice, and a rest the file does not print is not drawn. Not drawn: ties, and anything on the Metronome tab's staff or in the rudiments (they have no rests). It is made for judging timing, not for engraving.

6. The Rudiments tab

The Rudiments tab, between Score and Metronome, teaches the PAS international drum rudiments: all 40, grouped as rolls, diddles (paradiddles), flams and drags. The 26 that belong to the original NARD Standard 26 American Drum Rudiments are marked with *. A complete list with sticking is in the Rudiment reference.

A rudiment is drawn on the same two staves as a score, with the sticking (R and L) under each note, and everything you know from the Score tab works: Listen, Record, count-in, click, notes, loop, colours, Results. What is different is how you choose what to practise.

Choosing a rudiment

  • Prev and Next step through the list.
  • The button with the rudiment's name opens the list, grouped by family. Type in Search to narrow it (by name or number), and tick Only the original 26 (NARD) to list just those. Hover an entry for a one-line description. Rudiments you have played cleanly show their best tempo.
  • Below the title are the one-line description, the sticking written out (right-hand lead; in flams and drags a lower-case letter is the grace note) and the best clean tempo.

Repeats and lead

Instead of the Measures range, a rudiment has repeats: how many times the pattern is written one after the other, one bar each. That whole row of bars is the exercise. Press Listen with loop on to hear it endlessly while you get the feel.

Lead chooses which hand starts each repetition:

LeadMeaning
RightEvery repetition starts with the right hand (the way the pattern is written).
LeftThe mirror image: every repetition starts with the left hand.
AlternateRight, then left, then right again…

Each rudiment starts with the lead that lets its repetitions flow without playing one hand twice in a row at the join. A five stroke roll (R R L L R) therefore starts as Alternate (R-lead then L-lead), a paradiddle as Right (its pattern already contains both leads). You can still click a bar to narrow the range, as on the Score tab.

Tempo and progress

  • The tempo slider is in BPM (quarter notes, 30–240). Each rudiment starts at a tempo giving about six strokes a second on its closest strokes, and the tempo is remembered for each rudiment separately. Starting tempo goes back to the suggested one.
  • Rolls are written on a 16th-note grid (the PAS writes them in 32nds). A 16th at 120 BPM is the same speed as a 32nd at 60 BPM; you choose the speed with the tempo.
  • A clean take is a whole take of the exercise with an overall score of 85 or better. Its tempo is remembered as the best clean tempo of the rudiment.
  • With ladder ticked, the tempo goes up by 5 BPM after every clean take (as long as you played it at the current tempo or faster), so you climb as long as you play cleanly.
The PAS advicePractise every rudiment open (slow) to close (fast) to open (slow), or at an even moderate march tempo. The tempo slider, loop and ladder are made for exactly that.
Buzz rollThe multiple bounce roll (rudiment 4) is one held note. A drum module cannot be graded stroke by stroke on a buzz, so Record is switched off for it. You can still listen to it.

How rudiments are graded

Grading works as for a score (How grading works), with differences made for fast, repeated patterns:

  • The hit window follows the notes. The usual ±100 ms would reach several strokes of a fast rudiment. For rudiments the window is 45 % of the distance to the neighbouring strokes (never below your perfect tolerance), so a hit can never be within reach of two strokes at once.
  • Hands are compared. A drum module cannot tell which hand struck, but the expected hand of every stroke is known. The Results tab has a Hands table (strokes hit, average offset, average velocity of unaccented strokes for each hand), and the feedback tells you when one hand is behind the other or plays softer.
  • Flams and drags are grace notes. The soft grace notes are expected 35 ms before the main stroke (a drag's earlier note 70 ms). Playing them is never required and never counts as an extra hit; skipping them is not a miss.
  • Accents are graded by velocity like accents in a score.
Mesh padsVery fast diddles on a mesh pad can double-trigger and appear as extra hits. If you see that, check the pad's sensitivity and retrigger settings on the module.

Reading the notation

7. The Metronome tab

A click track that needs no score. Press Start (or M) and set:

  • tempo 20–300 BPM, beats per bar 1–12, and the subdivision: none, eighths, triplets or sixteenths; volume.
  • faster by (off by default) and every: a tempo ramp, +1 to +20 BPM every 1 to 32 bars, up to three times the starting tempo. It runs from the next start, and the beat lights and the rolling staff follow it.
  • The first beat of each bar is accented and subdivisions are softer. One circle per beat lights up as it sounds (the first in red).
  • Changes while it runs take effect after a moment.

The metronome plays through the click output chosen on Settings → Sound (the sound card and/or MIDI) and uses the click volume.

Rolling preview

Below the lights, the rolling preview scrolls notes past a fixed line, so you can play along with the click. With a score or rudiment loaded it shows the practice range (repeating at the metronome's tempo, the first note arriving on the first beat); with none it shows the click pattern of the bar. Match score tempo and bar sets the tempo and the beats per bar to the score's so the click lines up with the bars. Show the notes hides the preview.

CSV export

9. Settings

Settings are saved automatically whenever they change. The tab has five pages.

MIDI input

  • Input port, Refresh ports, Disconnect.
  • The monitor of incoming notes (latest first): channel, note, velocity, hi-hat pedal value and the instrument it maps to or unmapped.
  • Hi-hat pedal value when closed (see Hi-hat pedal).
  • The start trigger (below).
  • The pad-to-instrument mapping with Learn and Reset to Roland V31 defaults.

Start trigger

Starts a recording from the kit, so you do not have to let go of the sticks. Tick Start recording with the two hits and choose the first hit and the second hit instruments from the lists, or press Learn next to one and hit the pad. Then, on the Score or the Rudiments tab, hit the first instrument and, within half a second, the second one, with nothing else played in between: the recording starts with its count-in, exactly as if you had pressed R.

  • The two hits are not graded; they are no part of the take.
  • It does nothing on other tabs, and while a take is recording (so a crash and a tom in the music cannot restart it).
  • A wrong order, a late second hit, or a hit of another instrument in between starts the wait over. Hitting the first instrument again restarts the half second.
  • Under the score a grey line names the pair; it turns green while the program waits for the second hit.
  • Choose instruments you do not play right before you start, for example a crash followed by a tom. The same instrument twice is allowed too.
  • It is off until you switch it on. A closed (buzz) roll in the Rudiments tab cannot be recorded, so the trigger says so instead.

Controller

Choose the control surface (grouped as Mackie Control and Generic MIDI), its port and see what each control is bound to. Manage, next to the preset selector, opens the window for creating, copying, renaming and deleting presets and for teaching controls; details in Hardware controllers.

Timing

Sound

  • Audio output: the sound card used for the click, Listen and calibration. A change applies the next time sound starts; with System default chosen that includes a change of the default output in Windows (a Listen that is already playing stays on the output it began on). If the chosen device is not connected, the system default is used and a message says so. Once a sound has played, the page shows the open output, its rate and the delay the driver reports (only the least: the real delay is longer, and the page shows your calibrated latency next to it), and counts sounds that came too late (the computer is too busy for the output). The click and the built-in drums start at their exact moment, so their timing does not wobble with the size of the audio buffer.
  • MIDI output: the port that receives the click and the notes of Listen when they are sent over MIDI, such as the V31 or Windows' Microsoft GS Wavetable Synth.
  • Metronome: click through the sound card (with volume) and/or send it to the MIDI output (note and channel). Sending the click to the V31 (channel 10, an unmapped note such as 76, a wood block) puts it in your headphones with no computer-audio latency.
  • Listening: play Listen through the sound card (built-in drum sounds, volume) and/or send the notes to the MIDI output on a channel (default 10), so the V31 plays its own kit.
  • Instruments: the mixer (see Sound and instrument mixer).

General

  • Expand repeats and 1st/2nd endings: unrolls repeats when a score is loaded (on by default). Changing it reloads the file.
  • Recent scores: how many are remembered and Clear list.
  • Computer keyboard plays pads, with the key list.
  • The path of the settings file.

10. Hardware controllers (Mackie Control and MIDI)

A control surface runs the program from the stool, with sticks in your hands: record, listen, stop, the tempo, the measures, the click and the instrument mixer. Two kinds of controller are supported, and every preset says which it is:

  • Mackie Control: the Behringer X-Touch Mini (set it to MC mode) and X-Touch Compact. Layers, endless encoders, button lights and LED rings follow the Mackie Control protocol.
  • Generic MIDI: any controller that sends plain notes, controller numbers (cc) and pitch bend: knobs and faders that send their position, buttons that send a note or a cc. Built in for the Novation Launch Control XL, Korg nanoKONTROL2 (CC mode), Akai MPK Mini and Arturia BeatStep; for anything else you teach the program by pressing the controls (Managing presets and teaching controls).
Not tried on a deviceThe presets are written from the controllers' tables as remembered, and have not been tried on real controllers. A web search confirmed only a few of the generic numbers. If a number is wrong, the monitor shows the real one: copy the preset and fix the number or learn the control again.

Choosing a controller

  1. Open Settings → Controller and choose the preset from the list. The X-Touch Mini is chosen until you choose another, or — none —. The page shows the type (Mackie Control or Generic MIDI) under the connection message.
  2. The program finds the controller's MIDI port by the name in the preset; you can pick the port by hand too. The choice is remembered.
  3. The controller has its own MIDI port, separate from the drum module, so its buttons never count as hits. If you pick the drum module's port by mistake, the page says so. Close other programs that use the controller's port: on Windows a port can be opened by one program only.
  4. Plug the controller in after the start, or pull it out: it connects or lets go by itself (the port list is looked at every two seconds).

The Controller page has a monitor showing every message the controller sends and what it is bound to. The top bar shows the controller and, on the Mini, the layer in use.

The help screen

Click the controller at the top right of the window (⏺ X-TOUCH MINI, or ⏺ no controller in red) to open a picture of it, also when it is not connected. Every control is labelled with what it does on the layer shown; choose the layer with A and B (clicking the drawn LAYER buttons works too). Hover a control to read what it does and which MIDI message it sends. A green dot on a button means its light follows the program; amber dots around an encoder mean its LED ring shows the value.

The X-Touch Mini and the X-Touch Compact have pictures drawn after the devices. Any other controller, including one you taught yourself, gets a picture made from its preset: knobs and encoders on top, then faders, then buttons, in order of their message numbers. That picture does not show where the controls are on the device; it shows what the preset binds.

Managing presets and teaching controls

The Manage button next to the preset selector opens a window of its own. On the left are all presets, grouped as Mackie Control and Generic MIDI; built-in ones are marked. A click chooses a preset (it becomes the one in use, because teaching a control needs its connection) and shows its assignments on the right. Under the list:

  • Add makes a new, empty preset (My controller) for the controller's port. It is a Generic MIDI preset.
  • Copy makes a copy of the chosen preset, built in or not, and chooses it. The built-in presets cannot be changed, renamed or deleted; a copy can.
  • Delete… removes one of your own presets after asking once more.

On the right, the preset's name (type Rename and click the button) and a table of the program's actions: transport and practice, the values (knobs, encoders, faders) and the mixer. Each row shows the control bound to it, and:

  • Learn: click it, then press or turn the control on the device. The program binds the next message it receives. By default it then waits for the control of the next action in the list; untick After learning a control, go on to the next action to learn one at a time. Learning needs the controller to be connected, and while a control is being learned nothing else happens when you touch the device.
  • Layers: above the table, Add layer… asks for a name of one word and then for the button of the device that switches to it (press it; a button that already does something is refused with the reason). The layer is then shown in the table, ready for its own assignments. Delete layer asks twice and removes the layer with everything assigned on it; what works on every layer stays.
  • Lights and rings: the section under the table lists the actions that have a control. Add light makes the button light up on the same note or cc it sends, Add ring makes an LED ring on the same cc as the knob; change the number if your device uses another (the Mackie ring of an encoder is a different cc), and for a light the values that light it and darken it (often the colour). Whether the device does it is something only the device can tell: press the button, and watch.
  • Clear takes the control away. A control that already belonged to another action is taken from it, and the window says so.
  • Kind (for values): absolute for a knob or fader that sends its position, or relative for an endless encoder with the way it counts: mackie (1 to 63 clockwise, 65 to 127 counter-clockwise), twos (two's complement), offset (64 is no turn) or signbit2 (the opposite sign bit to Mackie). If a turn goes the wrong way or does nothing, try another, or click Detect next to the kind: turn the encoder slowly four clicks to the right, then four to the left, and the program chooses the encoding that fits (it says so, or says that it could not tell, for example for a knob that sends positions). The monitor shows the numbers. The recent scores and the rudiments are only turned, so they are always relative.
  • Limit a learned control to the MIDI channel it sends on (a tick box) is for controllers whose pads and knobs share numbers on different channels.

Every change is written to the preset file at once and takes effect without reconnecting. Editing writes the file afresh, so the comments of a copied preset are lost. A preset with layers has a layer selector at the top of the table. Not available in the window yet: creating layers, and editing button lights and LED rings (change the file by hand, see The preset file).

A preset is a text file ending in .txt in the controllers folder next to the settings file (the Manage window shows the path); it then appears in the list. The window writes these files for you; you can also write or change one by hand with any text editor, for what the window cannot do (layers, lights, rings). Without a kind line a file counts as Mackie Control if it has layers or lights, and as Generic MIDI otherwise. The relative encodings are mackie (the default), twos, offset and signbit2; ch N limits a line to one MIDI channel (a line for the channel wins over one for any channel); on= and off= are the values that light and darken a button, scale= the largest value of a ring.

Not supported: motor faders, scribble strip or time display, a second (XT) unit, blinking lights, system-exclusive set-up messages.

11. How grading works

Matching hits to notes

  • Each written note gets at most one hit and each hit at most one note. All pairs within the hit window are candidates; a hit on the same instrument costs its distance in time, a hit on a related instrument costs 40 ms more. Pairs are assigned closest first.
  • Related instruments are snare / cross-stick, hi-hat closed / open, ride / bell and the crashes. A hit on one of them at the right time counts as wrong pad with partial credit.
  • Notes not yet reached when you stop are not graded. Grace notes and flams are optional: skipping them is no miss, playing them is no extra.

Grades and credit

GradeCondition (defaults)Credit
Perfect≤ 20 ms1.00
Good≤ 45 ms0.85
OKinside the hit window (100 ms)0.60
Wrong padright time, related instrument0.30
Missedno hit0

Overall score

The 0–100 score is 55 % note accuracy + 25 % timing (average distance and spread) + 10 % no extra hits + 10 % dynamics. If the score has no accents or ghost notes, the dynamics share goes to the other three. Timing credit only counts as far as notes were played. Letters: A+ ≥ 95, A ≥ 90, B ≥ 80, C ≥ 70, D ≥ 60, otherwise E.

Dynamics: an accent should be at least 10 velocity points louder than the plain notes of that instrument, a ghost note at least 15 softer.

12. Latency and calibration

Every audio and MIDI path adds some delay: the module, USB, the program, and the sound card that plays the click. If you play exactly on the click you hear, your hit still arrives a little late. Roll Model subtracts a latency compensation from every hit to correct that.

  1. Open Settings → Timing and press Calibrate….
  2. Sixteen clicks play at 100 BPM. Hit any pad exactly with each click (the first four are for getting into the groove).
  3. The median offset becomes the latency compensation. At least six of the hits must lie within a quarter of a second of a click, otherwise calibration fails (is the click audible?).
Lower latencySending the click to the V31 (Settings → Sound → Metronome, channel 10, an unmapped note) puts it in your headphones without computer-audio latency. Calibrate again after changing the click path.
BluetoothBluetooth headphones and speakers add 100–200 ms or more of delay, and it is not steady. They cannot be used for timing: use a wired output, or send the click to the V31.

The compensation depends on how the click reaches your ears, so it is tied to the program's audio engine. When a new version changes the engine, Settings → Timing shows an amber note that the latency was set with an earlier one: press Calibrate… again, or Keep this value if you know it is still right. Moving the slider by hand counts as setting it with the current engine.

13. Sound and instrument mixer

What you hear

  • Computer sound output (default): a small built-in drum kit, synthesised on the fly. No sample files and no hardware are needed. It is meant for hearing rhythm and instrument, not for sounding like a studio kit.
  • MIDI output: each instrument is sent as its standard General MIDI / Roland note (or the note you mapped to it) on the chosen channel, to the chosen port. This works with the V31 or Windows' built-in synth. Both outputs can be on at the same time.

Instrument mixer

On Settings → Sound, every instrument has a volume, a mute and a solo (solo is not saved), live while Listen plays, and a play button that sounds it on its own. An open hi-hat is cut off when the hi-hat is closed.

Your own sounds

Put a WAV file named after the instrument in the sounds folder next to the settings file (the Sound page shows the path): Kick.wav, Snare.wav, CrossStick.wav, HiHatClosed.wav, HiHatOpen.wav, HiHatPedal.wav, Tom1.wav to Tom4.wav, Crash1.wav, Crash2.wav, Ride.wav, RideBell.wav, China.wav, Splash.wav, Cowbell.wav. Any instrument without a file keeps its built-in sound; a file that cannot be read is reported and replaced by the built-in one.

14. MusicXML scores

Supported

Partwise MusicXML 2.0–4.0, plain (.musicxml, .xml) or compressed (.mxl): multiple voices (backup/forward), chords, tempo changes (sound tempo or metronome marks), time-signature changes, pickup bars, repeats with 1st/2nd endings (expanded by default), ties, accents, ghost notes (parenthesised noteheads), grace notes and notehead shapes.

Instruments are recognised, in this order, from the General MIDI number in the file (midi-unpitched), the instrument name, or the staff position and notehead.

Not supported

  • D.S., D.C. and coda jumps are not followed.
  • score-timewise files are rejected.
  • With several parts, only the first part that looks like drums is used.
Export tipExport only the drum part and make sure instruments have their General MIDI numbers (most notation programs do this when you use their drum kit staff). If an instrument is shown on the wrong line, check its name and notehead in the source program.

17. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to try
The top right shows no MIDI inputIs the module on and connected? Close other programs that use the port (Windows allows one at a time). Open Settings → MIDI input, press Refresh ports and choose the port.
Hits show on the monitor as unmappedThe pad sends a note that is not in the map. Use Learn to assign it.
Hits are drawn but always early or late by the same amountRun the latency calibration. A steady offset is latency, not your playing.
Hits are wrong when you hit the hi-hatCheck the pedal value in the monitor and Hi-hat pedal value when closed.
No click or no sound in ListenIs the click / playback ticked on Settings → Sound? Is the right output device chosen? An amber message says when the chosen device is not connected. The click switch (K) must be on; the count-in always sounds.
Cymbals sound oddThe built-in kit is simple. Use your own WAV files or send Listen to the module over MIDI.
Many extra hits on fast rudimentsA mesh pad may double-trigger; check the module's retrigger settings. Slow the tempo.
A score loads but looks wrongSee MusicXML scores. Check instrument names and General MIDI numbers in the source program, and export only the drum part.
Bars are numbered strangelyWith repeats expanded, printed bar numbers repeat. The sliders and the Layout menu use the position in the score as shown; untick Expand repeats to see the file as written.
The controller does nothingIs it in Mackie Control (MC) mode? Is the right preset chosen? Watch the monitor on the Controller page: (not bound) means the number differs from the preset.
Learn does nothing, or the window says the controller is not connectedLearning goes through the connected controller: check the connection message and the port on the Controller page, then try again. The preset chosen must be one of your own (copy a built-in one first).
A turn of an encoder goes the wrong way or is ignoredThe preset reads it with another encoding. In the Manage window change absolute or relative and the encoding for that action, and watch the monitor.
The splash stays for a long time while startingIt lists the step it is waiting for, usually opening a MIDI port or listing the sound outputs. A device that is plugged in but not responding can hold it up; unplug it, or close the program that has its port.
The score scrolls and I cannot scroll back during a takeUntick follow.

19. Glossary

Absolute / relative
How a knob reports itself: its position (absolute), or how far it was turned (relative, an endless encoder).
Accent
A stroke played louder than its neighbours.
BPM
Beats per minute; here always quarter notes.
Count-in
Clicks before the music starts, so you can join in on time.
Diddle
A double stroke: two equal strokes with one hand.
Drag (ruff)
Two soft grace notes before a main stroke, played with the other hand.
Flam
A soft grace note an instant before a main stroke, played with the other hand.
Ghost note
A very soft note, shown in parentheses.
Hit window
How far from a note's time a hit may be to count for that note.
Layer
One of the sets of controls a controller switches between (the X-Touch Mini has A and B).
Lead
The hand that starts a pattern.
Learn
Teaching a controller by pressing its controls: the next message it sends is bound to the chosen action.
Mackie Control
A protocol for control surfaces, used by the X-Touch devices in MC mode.
NARD
National Association of Rudimental Drummers, source of the Standard 26 American Drum Rudiments.
PAS
Percussive Arts Society, which published the 40 International Drum Rudiments.
Preset
A text file that says which control of a controller does what; Mackie Control or Generic MIDI.
Rudiment
A basic sticking pattern from which drum technique is built.
Sticking
Which hand plays which stroke, written R and L.
Take
One recorded attempt over a range of bars, or over a rudiment.
